# Combat

This document specifies combat: the eight unit kinds and their roles, the counter triangle, the Watchtower and Town shooters, deterministic target acquisition, the seeded fixtures the counter tests run on, and a summary of movement. Combat in Eonmark is meant to be readable: a small roster, hard counters, no random damage by default. Stat values (hit points, damage, range, speed, cooldown, cost, pop) are not fixed by the design; they are set in `data/rules/units.ron` and `data/rules/buildings.ron` at M4a and must satisfy the seeded counter tests below. For the design lineage see [prior-art.md](prior-art.md).

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Milestone | M4a (sim), M4b (health bars, unit looks, attack-move key) |
| Labels | `area:sim`, `area:rules`, `kind:balance` for multiplier tweaks |
| Rules files | `data/rules/units.ron` (8 kinds, classes, stats, trainer, counter table in permille), `data/rules/buildings.ron` (Watchtower and Town shooter stats, HP), `data/rules/rules.ron` (`damage_variance_permille`, acquisition settings) |
| Sim modules | `crates/sim/src/combat.rs`, `crates/sim/src/movement.rs` |
| Sub-hash | `units`, `buildings`, `rng` |

## Counter triangle

Damage from attacker to defender is scaled by a permille multiplier from the counter table in `units.ron`. Entries not listed are 1000 (x1.0). The design fixes which pairs are counters, not the multiplier values.

| Attacker | Bonus against |
| --- | --- |
| Skirmisher | Bowman |
| Bowman | Shieldbearer |
| Shieldbearer | Outrider |
| Outrider | Bowman, Skirmisher, Mangonel |
| Mangonel | buildings |

The multiplier values must make each 10 vs 10 matchup below win at least 18 of 20 seeds. Proposing tweaks with a `cargo test -p sim counters` table is a good first issue.

## Damage

```text
raw      = attacker.damage (after Arms Mul 1100 per level, see tech-and-ages.md)
counter  = counter_table[attacker.kind][defender.kind]        // permille, default 1000
variance = 1000 + rng.range(-damage_variance_permille ..= damage_variance_permille)
damage   = floor(floor(raw * counter / 1000) * variance / 1000)
defender.hp -= damage
```

`damage_variance_permille` defaults to 0 in `rules.ron`, so combat is exactly reproducible from stats alone and stays readable. Setting it above 0 draws from the single `Pcg32` in the sim, which is hashed, so replays still verify. Hits land immediately in the sim; projectiles are render-side only (art pass: `crates/game/src/combat_fx.rs` draws an arrow, a stone or a melee lunge for every `SimEvent::Hit`, chosen from the attacker's class and range, and never writes to the sim). Cooldowns are authored in deciseconds (`_ds` fields) and converted once by `Rules::ticks_from_ds`.

## Shooters

Watchtowers and Towns attack on their own. Their range, damage and cooldown are in `buildings.ron`.

| Shooter | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Watchtower | Outranges Skirmishers (acceptance test). Also a border source of radius 2 and the host of Harrying research. Destroyed at 0 HP. Costs Ore. |
| Town | Shoots back. Indestructible: at 0 HP it stays and becomes annexable (see [towns.md](towns.md)). |

## Buildings in combat

Every building has HP. A building at 0 HP is removed, except Towns. Removal frees its footprint in the cost grid, which bumps `cost_grid_generation` and clears the path cache (see [pathing.md](pathing.md)); a removed Watchtower is also removed from the territory field. Mangonels carry the building counter and are the intended answer to Watchtowers and Towns.

## Target acquisition

Units acquire targets deterministically.

```text
each tick, for each unit that is idle, attack-moving or holding a dead target (UnitId order):
    candidates = enemy units and buildings within acquisition range,
                 found through the spatial grid (2 x 2 tile cells)
    sort candidates by (dist_sq_i64, id)
    tied = candidates sharing the smallest dist_sq_i64
    target = if tied.len() == 1 { tied[0] } else { tied[rng.range(0..tied.len())] }
```

The seeded tie-break is the only randomness in default combat. A unit keeps its target until the target dies, leaves range or the unit receives a new order. `Attack { units, target }` chases a specific target; `AttackMove { units, target }` moves toward a point and engages anything acquired on the way, then resumes. `Stop` clears the target. The attack-move key is `A` in the game (M4b).

## Fixtures and the 20-seed tests

Counter tests spawn 10 vs 10 of two kinds on an open field with a seeded +/- 1 tile spawn jitter per unit. The seed perturbs jitter and acquisition tie-breaks, so results differ across seeds, and the test asserts that they do. Each matchup runs 20 seeds.

| Matchup | Expectation |
| --- | --- |
| 10 Skirmishers vs 10 Bowmen | Skirmishers win at least 18 of 20, with at least 4 survivors |
| 10 Bowmen vs 10 Shieldbearers | Bowmen win at least 18 of 20 |
| 10 Shieldbearers vs 10 Outriders | Shieldbearers win at least 18 of 20 |
| 10 Outriders vs 10 Bowmen | Outriders win at least 18 of 20 |
| Watchtower vs Skirmishers | the Watchtower outranges them |

## Movement summary

Movement is specified in [pathing.md](pathing.md); the parts combat depends on:

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Pathing | Budgeted in-house A* on the 128 x 128 cost grid; repath every 60 ticks while moving or when the next waypoint becomes blocked. |
| Arrival steering | Fixed-point steering toward the next waypoint with slow-down at the goal; no trig, direction vectors only. |
| Separation | Push away from neighbours found in the 2 x 2 tile spatial grid, rebuilt each tick. |
| Circle correction | Circle-vs-circle overlap resolved in `UnitId` order so the result is independent of iteration luck. |
| Group orders | Square-spiral offsets assigned in `UnitId` order, skipping blocked tiles and tiles outside the click's connected component. |

Units without a path yet wait in place; nothing in combat depends on wall-clock time.

## M4a implementation notes

The combat core slice (`SIM_VERSION` 13, `rules_version` 15) implements hit points, the explicit `Attack`, simultaneous hits, death and building destruction in `crates/sim/src/combat.rs`, on the stats of the M4a rules data (`rules_version` 13). The combat behaviour slice (`SIM_VERSION` 14, `rules_version` 16) adds target acquisition by idle and attack-moving units, a real `AttackMove` (applied like `Move` until then), the Watchtower and Town shooters, seeded spawn jitter (`DebugSpawnJittered`), the 20-seed counter tests and the `combat_skirmish` golden. It changed no stat: the starting data passes every counter test (tables below). The M4a close adds the combat bench (below) and, from review, makes the direct-chase test trace the straight segment the chase walks from the unit's position (`combat::segment_clear`) instead of the line between tile centres, so a guard beside a blocked corner no longer drops and retakes its target; `SIM_VERSION` stays 14 and `rules_version` 16, because no fixture hash, counter table or bench result moved and `SIM_VERSION` 14 has not been released. Where it is more precise than the sections above:

| Topic | As implemented |
| --- | --- |
| State | `Unit` gains `damage_taken: i32`, `ready_at: u32` (the first tick it may hit again), `target: Option<TargetId>` and `intent: Intent` (`Plain`, `AttackMove { goal }`, `Attack`), hashed in `units`; `Building` gains `ready_at` for the shooters, hashed in `buildings`, and its `hp` (hashed since M3a) now takes damage. A unit's hit points are not stored: its maximum is resolved every tick from the kind's `hp` and the owner's `UnitHp` rows (at least 1), and it has `max - damage_taken`, so an Arms level raises a wounded unit's hit points as much as its maximum. Its damage is the attack's `damage` with the owner's `UnitDamage` rows (at least 0). `SimView::unit_stats(p, kind)` (hit points, damage, range, reach, cooldown) and `unit_hp(id)` (current and maximum) call the same functions as the step. |
| Tick order | Step 5 rebuilds the spatial grid, then 5a `combat::think` (every unit with an attack, in `UnitId` order, from the start-of-tick positions: an explicit `Attack` as below, an idle guard or an attack-mover acquiring; then every shooter, in `BuildingId` order), 5b `movement::step` with the engagements 5a decided, 5c `combat::resolve` (the units' hits, then the shooters', then the dead and the destroyed); see [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md). Nothing combat decides is kept across ticks except the fields above (a shooter keeps no target: its aim lasts the tick), so a restore has nothing new to rebuild. |
| Range metric | Edge to edge, compared in `dist_sq_i64`'s scale with no square root: a unit is in weapon range of a unit target when `dist_sq(a, t) <= (range + r_a + r_t)^2`, and of a building or site when the squared distance to the nearest point of its footprint rectangle, the tiles' area `[x0, x1 + 1] x [y0, y1 + 1]`, is at most `(range + r_a)^2`. With the starting data a melee attacker (range 0.25 tiles) and a target of radius 0.35 each are in range at 0.95 tiles centre to centre. |
| `Attack` | Check order, the first failure wins: (1) the target exists, is not the player's and, for a building, has hit points left, so a complete Town at 0 HP is not a target (`InvalidTarget`); (2) at least one listed unit is the player's and of a kind with an attack (`NoValidUnits`). An accepted `Attack` gives each such unit, in `UnitId` order, the target and `Intent::Attack` and drops its order, post, job and path request; the chase starts in step 5a of the same tick. `Move`, `AttackMove`, `Stop`, `Gather` and `Build` clear the target. An `Attack` draws nothing from the RNG, accepted or rejected (until this slice it was rejected with `NotImplemented` and drew once). `SimView::check_attack` makes the same checks after the player checks. |
| Chase and give-up | Each tick in 5a, from the start-of-tick positions. A target that is gone, the player's own or a building at 0 HP ends the order: no target, `Plain`, no order, no path request. In weapon range the unit holds: no desired velocity, the idle separation push cap, facing the target. Within its reach (the longer of `acquire_range_tiles`, 6, and its weapon range, edge to edge) and with a clear straight segment from the unit's position to the target, or to the nearest point of a building's footprint (every tile the segment passes through is passable, a corner crossing needs both tiles beside the corner, and the target's own footprint does not count; the segment is the walk the direct chase steers, so while the target stands still the test holds from every point the chase reaches, and the test that takes a target is the one that keeps it), or, for a building, from any tile beside its footprint (the nearest point of the footprint then lies on that tile's own edge or corner, so the straight steer never leaves the tile; a building's path chase ends on such a tile, and from a corner one the segment can be blocked by a neighbour flush against the footprint), it chases directly: it steers straight at the target, or at the nearest point of the footprint, at full speed with the moving push cap, with no path and no request; a path-chase order is dropped with its request first, because step 4 writes a completed path into whatever order a unit has. Otherwise it walks a path, which ends on: the target's tile when the unit can walk there (the same connected component); else the tile of the unit's component nearest to the target (the tile pathing corrects the goal to, `Map::nearest_passable`) provided the target is in weapon range from anywhere within the unit's arrive radius of that tile's centre, so a Bowman walks to the near side of a wall or river and shoots across while a melee unit never hits a target walled off by a whole tile; for a building, the reachable ring tile nearest to the unit, as a builder's. With no such end tile the target cannot be reached and the order ends: at once, with no path, and on any later tick of the walk (a wall can close round the target), the cheap checks first (a target the unit can walk to, one it can hit from the end of its current path, a ring tile still in its component) and the search only when they fail. It is judged from the map, the two positions and the stats alone, so every attacker of a group gives up in the same tick; on a path each, a group would all walk to the one corrected goal tile, which not all of them can come within the arrive radius of, and the rest would jostle there for ever. With no order (and no post) the unit walks to the target's tile (a building's ring tile). With an order it re-goals to the target's tile once that is more than `chase_regoal_tiles` (2, Chebyshev) from the goal (to a new ring tile when a building's is cut off) and no request is outstanding. A re-goal is a repath: the unit keeps walking its current path until step 4 writes the new one, and while any request is outstanding it does not arrive at the end of its path but waits there (arriving would end the order, step 4 would drop the new path and the chase would start over from the back of the queue). An explicit `Attack` has no leash. A unit whose path chase has arrived (no order, a post) while its target is still neither in range nor directly chaseable walks again when the end tile is no longer the tile it arrived at (a target that stopped within `chase_regoal_tiles` of the goal round an obstacle, or walked on), and otherwise gives up; each such walk needs the target to have moved. A path step 4 finds unreachable (`PathUnreachable`) ends the order too. So an unreachable target costs no path, and one walled off during the chase no more than the path being walked, never a request every tick. |
| Hits | Step 5c collects, in `UnitId` order, every unit whose target is still valid and in weapon range from the post-movement positions and whose `ready_at` has come. Each hit does `floor(floor(raw * counter / 1000) * variance / 1000)` in `i64` (`raw` with the Arms rows; `counter` the listed row for the pair, `Buildings` for any building or site, else 1000; `variance` 1000 with no draw at `damage_variance_permille` 0, else one `rng_index(2v + 1)` draw per hit in collection order), sets the attacker's `ready_at` to the tick plus its cooldown and emits `Hit { attacker, target, damage }`. The hits are summed per target and applied together: they are simultaneous, so a unit killed this tick still lands its own and a lower id has no first strike. Overkill is accepted: no attacker skips a target already dealt enough this tick. |
| Death | Every unit whose `damage_taken` has reached its maximum is removed, in `UnitId` order, with its path request (`UnitDied { unit }`). The slot it worked, the site it built and the pop it used are derived, so they are free at once. |
| Destruction | Then every building or site at 0 HP or below other than a complete Town is removed, in `BuildingId` order (`BuildingDestroyed { building }`): no refund, its queue is lost (the pop it charged is free again, and a tech it was researching can be queued again), its builder loses its job and a complete Watchtower's border source leaves the territory field (one `TerritoryChanged`; see [territory.md](territory.md)). Sites of every kind are destroyed, Town sites included (owner decision 2026-10-21). Every footprint destroyed in a tick is unblocked by one `Map::set_blocked_many`, one cost-grid generation bump, so the path cache is cleared once and a suspended search is requeued on the new grid. Each Town a destroyed building was attributed to is then re-derived (`town::refresh`, in id order) and may fall back to tier 1. |
| Towns at 0 HP | A complete Town is clamped at 0 HP and stays, and is no longer a target (owner decision 2026-10-21): it keeps its footprint, border and queue, emits `TownAtZeroHp { town }` once, on the hit that brings it to 0, and its attackers stand down the next tick. It does not regenerate (the M4a plan's choice, see [towns.md](towns.md) open question 2) and does not shoot. `Train` there is still accepted until M5a's annexation. Annexation needs infantry or cavalry next to such a Town; an idle soldier acquires the nearest enemy in its reach, which may draw it away from the Town, and M5a has to allow for that (see [towns.md](towns.md)). |
| Who acquires | A unit with an attack that is idle (no order and no job, with or without a post: a guard) or attack-moving (`Intent::AttackMove` while its order lasts). A plain `Move`, a rally walk, a job walk (no armed kind works a job) or a guard walking a path back to its post never engages; such a unit has no target. An explicit `Attack` never acquires: it keeps its ordered target until the order ends, then the unit is idle and guards from the next tick. |
| Acquisition | Candidates are the enemy units found through the spatial grid and the enemy buildings and sites with hit points left (so never a complete Town at 0 HP) that are within the unit's reach (the longer of `acquire_range_tiles`, 6, and its weapon range), edge to edge, and are in weapon range or directly chaseable: the explicit `Attack`'s test, a clear straight segment from the unit's position within the reach or, for a building, standing beside its footprint (`chases_directly`, shared by both, and the same for taking a target and for keeping it). An acquired target is never walked to along a path, so acquisition costs no path request and never searches for a reachable tile. Candidates rank by `(tier, out of weapon range, dist_sq)`: tier 0 is enemy units and tier 1 enemy buildings, the other way round for a kind with `prefer: Buildings` (the Mangonel); in weapon range comes before out of it; the squared distance is centre to centre for a unit and to the nearest point of the footprint for a building, so each tier compares like with like. Candidates that tie exactly on that key are put in `TargetId` order and one is drawn with `rng_index(n)`. The segment test runs lazily, in rank order. |
| Keeping and switching | A unit keeps its target while the target is valid and in weapon range or directly chaseable (so it is dropped beyond the reach, the leash for acquired targets) unless a candidate ranks strictly better on `(tier, out of weapon range)`: a target out of weapon range gives way to a candidate of its own tier in weapon range (the melee crowd case: a soldier that cannot get at its target fights the enemy next to it), and a building target to any qualifying enemy unit (for the Mangonel, a unit target to a building). A target of the better tier in weapon range is never displaced, and nothing is searched for it. Decided 2026-10-22 in the M4a combat behaviour slice, refining owner decision (3) (units before buildings, switching to an enemy in weapon range): the plan's rule, read literally, would let a building in weapon range displace a unit target out of range and would never let a unit displace a building in range, so a soldier could hit a Town for 2 a blow while enemy units cut it down. The owner may revisit. The kept target is held (in weapon range) or chased directly; nothing is drawn while a target is kept. |
| `AttackMove` | Applied like `Move` (the same spiral goals, path requests and rejections), then every unit of a kind with an attack gets `Intent::AttackMove { goal }`, its own spiral goal; unarmed units just move (so `m2.rs`'s Yeomen test still holds). While it fights, an attack-mover keeps its order without following it: it does not consume waypoints, arrive or repath; when the fight ends it resumes the path, and the periodic repath (due by then) corrects any detour. The intent ends with the order: the next think after `UnitArrived` finds no order and makes the unit `Plain` (it guards where it stands); step 4's `PathUnreachable` (defensive: pathing corrects every goal into the unit's component, so an attack-move onto an unreachable point arrives at the nearest reachable tile) clears it at once; `Move`, `Stop`, `Attack`, `Gather` and `Build` replace it. `combat::think` never issues an attack-move order itself. |
| Guards | An idle armed unit engages any enemy in its reach on its own, freshly spawned or trained units included; one with a post holds or chases and keeps the post, then walks back to it (return to post) once the target is dead or dropped. A `Stop` ends an explicit order or an attack-move, but a stopped unit is idle, so it takes the nearest enemy in reach again (with `Intent::Plain`). |
| Shooters | Every complete building whose kind has an `attack` (the Watchtower and the Town) and hit points left, whose `ready_at` has come, aims in 5a at the nearest enemy unit within `range + r_u` of its footprint (squared distance from the unit's centre to the footprint rectangle; exact ties drawn with `rng_index` in `UnitId` order) and fires in 5c if the unit is still in range after movement, then waits `cooldown_ds` (`ready_at`). It shoots units only, never buildings; no Modifier row and no counter row applies to a shot (counter 1000), and damage variance applies like to any hit. A site does not shoot, and neither does a complete Town at 0 HP (owner decision 2026-10-21); a Town's last shot lands in the tick it reaches 0, since hits are simultaneous. With the starting data a tower or Town hits a Yeoman or Skirmisher up to 7.35 tiles from its footprint, every 30 ticks, for 15. |
| Spawn jitter | `DebugSpawnJittered { owner, kind, at, count, jitter_x100 }`, appended to `Command` after `DebugResearch` and gated like `DebugSpawn`, spawns on `DebugSpawn`'s spiral (one `UnitSpawned` per unit), then for each new unit in id order draws `dx` and then `dy`, each `rng_index(2J + 1) - J` hundredths of a tile, and moves the unit off its tile centre by them; a point off the map, on a blocked tile or in another component than the tile keeps the centre, but the draws are made anyway. A rejection (`DebugCommandsDisabled`, `UnknownPlayer`, `UnknownUnitKind`) draws nothing. The counter duels and `combat_skirmish` use it with `jitter_x100: 100`. |
| RNG | Default combat draws only to break exact ties: one draw when an acquiring unit or a shooter meets candidates at exactly the same distance, in think order (units in `UnitId` order, then shooters in `BuildingId` order). Hits draw only for damage variance, one per hit in hit order, and the shipped `damage_variance_permille` is 0. `DebugSpawnJittered` draws two per unit. `rng_index(rng, n) = (next_u32 * n) >> 32`, which does not depend on the `rand` version. |
| Events | `UnitDied { unit, owner, kind }` and `BuildingDestroyed { building, owner, kind }` carry the owner and kind captured before the removal, so a presenter need not have seen the unit or building; a shot is a `Hit` whose `attacker` is `TargetId::Building`. |

### The combat bench (acceptance 4)

`cargo run -p sim-cli --release -- bench --units 400 --ticks 1200 --combat [--seed S]` runs `sim::scenarios::combat_bench(rules, units, seed)`, which is not in `NAMES` and never a fixture: an empty start on `plains_1v1` where each side spawns one `DebugSpawnJittered` group of `units / 8` per line kind (Skirmisher, Shieldbearer, Bowman, Outrider) in the all-grass west rectangle, P0's centred on (6, 28), (6, 36), (14, 28) and (14, 36) and P1's mirrored across x = 22, and during tick 1 each side attack-moves to the other's centre, (34, 32) and (10, 32). Only `Sim::step` is timed (`Instant` stays in `sim-cli`, never in the sim) and events are counted outside the timed region. The output is the overall step mean, p95 and max; the fighting ticks (a tick with at least one `Hit` after which both sides keep a quarter of their units, 50 at 400) with their mean and p95; the hits, the deaths per side and the buildings destroyed; and the final hash. It exits 1 only when fewer than 100 ticks were fighting ticks (seeds 1 to 10 give 454 to 501). `--combat` conflicts with `--astar` and `--territory` (`cli_parses_bench_combat_flags`), and `bench_combat_small_run_fights` runs 40 units for 300 ticks: hits, deaths on both sides and the same hash twice. The criterion bench `combat_step` (`cargo bench -p sim --bench combat_step`) snapshots the battle before its first fighting tick and times the 20 steps from there on a restored sim. Measured numbers are in `docs/BUILD_TIMES.md`; the run at the M4a close is under "Acceptance checklist" below. M5a adds `--harrying`, the same battle with attrition on (both players at Harrying II, a P1 Town beside the arena, a Supply Wain in each attack-move), which prints `attrition_pulses=`, `supplied=` and `exposed=` too ([attrition.md](attrition.md), "The Harrying bench").

## M5a notes (sim)

The M5a attrition slice (`SIM_VERSION` 15, `rules_version` 19) touches combat in three places; see [attrition.md](attrition.md), "M5a implementation notes".

| Topic | As implemented |
| --- | --- |
| Tick order | Step 5 is now 5a `think`, 5b `movement::step`, 5b' `attrition::step` and 5c `resolve`. Attrition runs on the post-movement positions and only adds to `damage_taken`; it removes nothing. |
| Attrition deaths | `resolve` ends in `reap` even when no hit lands, so a unit an attrition pulse takes to its maximum dies in the same step's 5c through the common death path: `UnitDied { unit, owner, kind }`, its path request cancelled, its slot, site and pop freed, with the hit points of the `Stats` built at the start of step 5. Such a unit still lands its own hit in 5c, as with simultaneous hits, and a shooter's aim at it still fires. |
| `apply_damage` | `resolve`'s per-target apply is `combat::apply_damage(state, target, amount, events)`: a unit's `damage_taken` grows (clamped to `i32`); a complete Town falls to at least 0 with `TownAtZeroHp` on the step that reaches 0; any other building or site loses hit points. It removes nothing; `reap` does. |
| `DebugDamage` | `Command::DebugDamage { target, amount }`, appended to `Command` after `DebugSpawnJittered` and gated like `DebugSpawn`, applies `amount` through `apply_damage` during step 3 (apply), with no attacker, range or counter and no `Hit` event; 5c then removes the dead and the destroyed. The target only has to exist (`InvalidTarget` otherwise), so any owner's unit or building qualifies, the issuer's own and a Town at 0 HP included, unlike `Attack`'s `locate`. It draws nothing from the RNG. Tests and scenarios use it to set hit points without depending on combat tuning (`attrition_debug_damage_kills_through_reap_and_clamps_towns`, `attrition_debug_damage_rejections_change_nothing`); `sim-cli fuzz` expects a `DebugDamage` on `UnitId(0)` to be rejected. |

The M5a annexation slice (`SIM_VERSION` 16, `rules_version` 20) adds the annex-hold guard rule (owner decision 6) and moves the rules-only tables out of the step; see [towns.md](towns.md), "M5a implementation notes".

| Topic | As implemented |
| --- | --- |
| Annex-hold guard rule | In 5a, an idle guard (no order, no job, `Intent::Plain`) whose kind `annexation_attackers` selects (infantry and cavalry) and that stands on a tile within `annexation_adjacent_tiles` (1) of an enemy complete Town at 0 HP keeps its target only while it is in weapon range, and `best_candidates` considers only targets in weapon range for it (`Want` is capped at `InRange`), so it only ever holds: nothing draws it off the ring. Everywhere else acquisition is unchanged; `think` collects the Towns at 0 HP once per step (usually none). `annexation_adjacent_guards_hold_the_ring` shows a Croft 3.5 tiles from a post left alone for 300 steps, and taken and chased by the same guards when the rules do not select their kind. None of the eight re-recorded fixtures' Towns ever loses a hit point (`capture_seat`, a new recording, takes P1's Seat to 0 and exercises annexation and the hold rule), so their units are identical tick for tick across the re-record, and `combat_skirmish_beats_happen` pins that its P1 Town keeps hit points. |
| Tick order | 5d `annexation::step` follows 5c, on the post-movement, post-reap positions, and 6e `victory::step` ends the step. A flipped Town gets 500 of its 2000 hit points back, so it shoots and can be targeted again from the next step. |
| Combat tables | The counter table, each kind's preference, the shooters, the largest unit radius and the variance depend on the rules alone: `combat::CombatTables` is built once in `Sim::new` and again in `Sim::rebuild_derived`, and each step's `Stats` borrows it and resolves only every player's unit stats. A pure refactor (every fixture verifies unchanged); it took the 54000-step stalemate test from 0.45 s to 0.29 s. |

## Open tuning questions

1. Counter multiplier values: the design fixes the pairs and the 18/20 bar, not the numbers. Decided 2026-10-21 (owner accepted the M4a plan defaults): the starting table in `units.ron` is Skirmisher on Bowman 2500, Bowman on Shieldbearer 3000, Shieldbearer on Outrider 3000, Outrider on Bowman 2500, on Skirmisher 1500 and on Mangonel 3000, Mangonel on buildings 4000, and the Skirmisher, Shieldbearer, Bowman and Outrider on buildings 250 each. The M4a combat behaviour slice measured it against the 18/20 bar: every matchup wins 20 of 20 (see "Counter tests"), so no tuning step was taken.
2. Trainer for Outrider (Muster Hall or Engine Yard) and for Scribe (Scriptorium or Town). The M3a data trains the Scribe at the Scriptorium. Decided 2026-10-21 by the M4a plan (an implementation choice, not one of the nine owner decisions in ROADMAP.md; the owner may revisit): the Outrider trains at the Muster Hall, the Mangonel and the Supply Wain at the Engine Yard, and the Scribe stays at the Scriptorium.
3. Is Bowman an infantry class for annexation purposes? This doc says yes. Decided 2026-10-21 by the M4a plan (an implementation choice, not one of the nine owner decisions in ROADMAP.md; the owner may revisit): yes, the Bowman's class is `Infantry` in `units.ron`.
4. Acquisition range versus weapon range: equal in the starting data, or a slightly larger acquisition range so units close in. Decided 2026-10-21 (owner accepted the M4a plan defaults): one global `acquire_range_tiles: 6` in `rules.ron`. By the M4a plan, a unit's reach is the longer of that and its weapon range, so the Bowman (5 tiles) reaches 6 and the Mangonel (8) 8; the reach bounds both direct chases and acquisition.
5. Chase leash for `Attack` on a fleeing target. Decided 2026-10-21 by the M4a plan (an implementation choice, not one of the nine owner decisions in ROADMAP.md; the owner may revisit): no leash for an explicit `Attack`; the order ends only when the target dies, is destroyed or becomes invalid, on a new order, or when the target cannot be reached (see "M4a implementation notes"). An acquired target is dropped once it is beyond the reach, or neither in weapon range nor directly chaseable (it is never walked to along a path).
6. Whether `damage_variance_permille` ever leaves 0 in a shipped ruleset. Decided 2026-10-21 by the M4a plan (an implementation choice, not one of the nine owner decisions in ROADMAP.md; the owner may revisit): it ships at 0, validated to 0..=500, so default combat draws nothing; a ruleset may raise it, and every hit then draws once from the sim's RNG.
